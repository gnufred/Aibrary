## Chapters 1 and 2 · The hourly break and the breathing experiments

**Fred Potvin, *My health hacks*, and his account for this book.** The article places the start of his search when his family was growing. He recalls feeling better within a year and gives two running milestones across two years. Six years describes his larger period of reading and trying things. On 25 November he identified his current favorite separately. Every hour he stops, moves for about ten seconds, then breathes in slowly and expands his upper chest as fully as he can. He wants to stretch his muscles, especially his upper back, and says the break helps him relax and feel better. This is the source for his sequence and experience. Describe improvements in his health as his recollections. [Pinned article, revision 2cd1155](https://github.com/gnufred/gnufred.github.io/blob/2cd1155d8174307b1014345cd99b41ef4d60e287/docs/health-hacks.html) · [Fred’s hourly practice as recorded for the book](../01-plan-book/plan.md#my-hourly-movement-and-breathing-break)

**James Nestor, *Breath* (2020).** Fred says this book made the possibility of a free change feel real. It led him to pay attention to breathing through his nose, including on runs. That is enough to explain its place at the start of the book. Galpin later describes nasal breathing as one option and discusses changing breathing patterns as exercise intensity rises. The article’s claim about reshaping an adult skull through chewing or nasal breathing has no matching clinical evidence in the sources checked for this story. Leave it out of the practical explanation. The publisher identifies the US edition. Fred’s own edition and the page that first caught his attention are open. [Nestor’s book page](https://www.mrjamesnestor.com/breath-book) · [US publisher](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/547761/breath-by-james-nestor/9780735213616/) · [Fred’s Galpin link at 5:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oNkDA2F7CjM&t=338s) · [Galpin on exercise intensity at 2:49:23](https://www.hubermanlab.com/episode/dr-andy-galpin-how-to-build-physical-endurance-and-lose-fat?timestamp=10163)

**Andrew Huberman, breathing episode and physiological sigh clip.** The short clip mattered because Fred could use the sigh before a stressful encounter. The longer episode discusses repeated sighing at 1:22:39. In Balban and colleagues’ 2023 trial, participants practiced for five minutes daily for a month. A closer comparison for the time Fred describes is a 2026 pilot with 47 participants. One minute of cyclic sighing or box breathing was associated with greater short-term reductions in self-reported anxiety than a passive control. These are studies of particular breathing exercises. Fred’s movement followed by a full inhalation remains his own practice. He recalls a 15% gain in running speed during that period. The starting pace, route, other training, and method of comparison are unknown. Keep that figure separate from the two-year running story. ## Chapter 3 · The first glass of water

The resting-water segment actually begins at 1:44:18. Disinfectants behave differently. For example, a water utility’s FAQ explains why letting a pitcher stand is impractical for removing chloramine. Keep Fred’s glass of water as the morning habit. Any claim about treatment needs the particular water supply, substance, and filter. The article credits Huberman Lab and the Wim Hof Method generally for the wake-up sequence. It gives no source assigning Fred’s exact sequence to either creator. The chapter can describe why Fred wanted a simple routine and leave the arithmetic out of the reader exercise. **Coffee.** Fred adopted a first-cup window and usually skipped weekends. Huberman describes personally delaying caffeine on some mornings and taking it earlier before certain workouts. Galpin’s separate passage concerns endurance. A controlled crossover study in 23 men tested caffeine doses at intervals **before bedtime**. The scene Fred can tell is how different that familiar beer looked when he thought in weeks. His account establishes a change of mind. It leaves his subsequent drinking history open. ## Chapter 4 · Five push-ups and a longer run

**Andy Galpin on short bouts of movement.** The endurance interview gave Fred a way to think about fitting exercise into a day. His sets of five push-ups and squats are his own example. A small randomized study by Jenkins and colleagues tested short stair climbs three days a week for six weeks in sedentary young adults and observed a fitness improvement. It lends context for brief bouts. Fred’s two reported runs imply average paces of 11:15/km and 5:00/km. Their different distances and conditions matter, and they give no estimate of the effect of any single habit. [Fred’s link at 8:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oNkDA2F7CjM&t=530s) · [Jenkins et al., stair-climbing trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30649897/)

**Scott Carney, *What Doesn’t Kill Us* (2017), and the cold shower.** Fred took an interest in training outdoors and trying cold showers after reading Carney. The book is a journalist’s account of testing methods associated with Wim Hof. Fred also names Huberman Lab and the Wim Hof Method generally here. His note identifies no specific passage for the claim that weather supplies half the benefit of training. Keep the outdoor preference and the shower as Fred experienced them. [Carney’s description of his reporting](https://www.scottcarney.com/what-doesnt-kill-us/)

One trial is especially relevant because it tested **showers**. Buijze and colleagues assigned 3,018 adults to end hot showers with cold water or to continue usual showers. The cold-shower groups reported fewer days absent from work. The groups showed no statistically significant difference in days feeling ill. That distinction matters alongside the article’s recollection of rarely getting sick. The trial used a different schedule from Fred’s and did not measure his running performance. A single sentence from the result is cleared if the author wants its exact wording. [Published trial](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0161749) · [Cleared sentence and attribution](sources/buijze-2016-illness-days.md)

**ATHLEAN-X, low-back video.** Fred linked a demonstration for a back that had seized up. The article gives no account of his diagnosis, using that particular movement, or how quickly he recovered afterward. Cite the demonstration as something he wanted to share. French clinical guidance on common low-back pain bases activity and exercise choices on the person’s presentation. A personal episode would need to come from Fred. [Original video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DWmGArQBtFI) · [Haute Autorité de santé, 2019 guideline](https://www.has-sante.fr/jcms/c_2961499/fr/lombalgie-commune)
